Coil Chiptune

Turns a song you own into a square-wave melody your Tesla coil can play.

1 · Choose a song

Get my iTunes song
Bought it in iTunes? Tap "Get my iTunes song" first. It copies the song to Files, then comes back here so you can choose it. For orchestral songs, a MIDI file of the melody plays exactly right on the coil.
First time? Set up "Song to Files" (2 min)
  1. In the Music app, tap the download arrow on your song.
  2. Open the Shortcuts app and tap +.
  3. Add action "Select Music".
  4. Add action "Encode Media", tap its arrow and turn Audio Only on.
  5. Add action "Save File".
  6. Name the shortcut exactly Song to Files and tap Done.

Songs streamed from Apple Music or Pandora can't be copied.

2 · Play it on the coil

Coil setup

  1. Turn on the coil and switch it to music mode.
  2. Phone Settings → Bluetooth → pick the coil (often named "Tesla coil 10").
  3. Set the coil's knob where it normally sounds good for you.
  4. Start the phone volume around the middle, press play, then raise it slowly. If you hear static, turn the phone volume down a few steps.
  5. Keep this page open while it plays.
Coil sound test (1 minute)

Play each clip through the coil. They all play the start of "Ode to Joy" (Beethoven, public domain), each encoded a different way. This shows which method your coil can actually play.

Tell Claude: which numbers sound like the tune, and whether 1 and 2 sound the same or different.

Your song never leaves this phone. Busy parts of a song may come out rough. Music with a clear melody on top (a voice, violins, flute) works best. Follow the coil's safety warnings.
